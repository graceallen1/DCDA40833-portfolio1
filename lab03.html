The US is Boosting Alzheimers Funding: Reflection

Information Resolution

The infographic I chose to critique is titled "The US is Boosting Alzheimers funding" and was created by the Alzheimers Impact Movement. The infographic the amount of spending from the US in 2011 compared to 2020. The infrographic showed this by having one big circle which represented 2020, showing that 2.8 billion was spent and inside that circle was a smaller circle representing 2011, showing that 0.5 billion was spent that year. At the top, it explained that this is a 525% increase between those nine years. After reading the article that was attatched to the infographic, I do think that the graph represented the incline well, but there were several other years in between that could have been represented as well. Another thing that I noticed is that the funding is for both alzheimers and dementia, which are very alike but I think it would have been useful to specify on the infographic too. Also, showing why there has been more funding, it made me wonder if there has been an increase in people being diagnosed with Alzheimers and Dementia or what the motivation for the increase was.

One concern that I had with the infographic that I have touched on was that it cherry picked from the article by only showing two years. I think that if the infographic showed the steady increase like the article shows, it would be more effective. Espeically since the infographic mentions the 525% increase over those years, it would make a lot of sense to show how it has increased. Similarly, only mentioning Alzheimers funding and not Alzheimers and Demetia and related health problems being funded.

Another concern about the infographic that I had was that the infographic failed to show why this funding was increased and why it is so important. The article talks more about how Alzheimers and Dementia funding makes an impact on patients and their families but leaves out specific information. Nothing about that is reflected in the infographic but also neither was why the funding was increased. The infographic has the effect, without the cause.

I think that overall, this infographic showed the little information it provided well, but could have included more information to make it stronger. The infographic cherry picked from the article and showed the result of the funding, but not what caused the increase in funding to happen.
